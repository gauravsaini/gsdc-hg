# Personal site prototype

Question: can the current Hugo Identity one-card page become a genuine personal internet corner with a cinematic Apple-like rhythm, without feeling like a consulting landing page?

This throwaway prototype is now locked to one direction: `?variant=a` — Personal room, warm, direct, and centred on “now”. The alternative scrapbook and quiet-portrait routes were removed after review.

Shared interaction hypothesis: use spring-like motion for buttons, portrait cards and the Dynamic Island-style status control; use scroll reveals for the personal “now” and history sections. Delete this folder after a direction is chosen, then absorb the winner into Hugo layouts/assets.

Personal room also includes a small rocket ship in the corner: launch it to travel between night and day worlds. Dark mode is the default, and light mode uses a softer paper/green palette rather than a simple colour inversion.

The opening sequence is intentionally treated as part of the artwork: a short gallery-style title card with a small launch vehicle, animated orbital lines, grain, and a progress counter. The Personal room hero and scroll chapters continue that visual language with a live generative canvas, ghost typography, orbital illustrations, and a constellation-like contact card.

Latest entry pass makes the arrival a threshold interaction: the room starts in near-darkness, the rocket ignites, a horizontal light cut and oversized G-sigil wake up, then the identity title is staggered into the reveal. Clicking the opening scene skips ahead after ignition.

The Personal room's “a little now” chapter was replaced with a public-project gallery sourced from `gh` metadata. Current picks: `kevin`, `bitresearch-mlx`, `chronoquant`, and `pager`. Private repositories and private commits are intentionally excluded; this prototype only links to public GitHub repositories.

The loader kicker now uses the user's deeper Sanskrit line verbatim, with a restrained English gloss beneath it so the opening feels personal and enigmatic rather than like a generic portfolio label.

The theme control is now a rocket ship. Launching it creates a short time-warp tunnel, regenerates the hero's stars and planets, and flips between night and day palettes. The status control now reads as a live Melbourne transmission with coordinates; the Indian/Australian identity is left implicit through the flags in the `127.0.0.1` mark.

The hero planets now travel on the same animated galaxy rings as the background: each planet shares a ring index, center, rotation, and wave calculation, so the foreground objects and orbital field stay visually locked together.

The rocket transition now uses one shared 2.4-second velocity curve: ignition, acceleration, peak warp, then gradual deceleration. The rocket animation, canvas speed-up, streaks, zoom, blur, and theme recovery all finish together.

During that jump, planets fade out through the warp and fade back in on arrival. The rocket’s visible day/night caption was removed so the control reads as a small spacecraft rather than a toggle.

The About chapter now treats `127.0.0.1 🇮🇳 🇦🇺` as a signature origin mark. The flags carry the heritage cue without spelling it out; the mark gets its own route-plaque styling in both dark and light themes.

The hero portrait is now circular, with a warm sun placed behind it at the shared orbital center. Background planets use restrained natural palettes inspired by Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn; only Saturn keeps a visible ring so the scene feels solar-system-like instead of neon sci-fi.

The orbital field now uses aligned, tilted ellipses with a gentle shared pulse. Planets follow their own angular speeds and independent directions, while Saturn is deliberately kept on the visible side of the outer orbit and gets a two-layer ring so the ring reads clearly.

Every generated scene now varies its orbit count, planet count, occupied orbit slots, palette order, and planet scale. Saturn remains guaranteed as the outer ringed planet, but the rest of the solar system composition changes on reload and rocket travel.

The longer version now identifies IAG (Insurance Australia Group) as the current workplace, while keeping the earlier Pepperstone, IRESS, and Accenture chapters as context.
